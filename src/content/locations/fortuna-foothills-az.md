Fortuna Foothills sits east of the city of Yuma, closer to the Gila Mountains, and has grown rapidly as a residential area over recent years — largely through newer, master-planned subdivisions rather than the older in-town neighborhood pattern found closer to central Yuma.

## Why block wall demand runs high here

A significant share of Fortuna Foothills' housing growth has come through HOA-governed developments, many of which specify masonry perimeter walls — consistent with City of Yuma-area code requiring subdivision perimeter fencing to be masonry with an anti-graffiti coating in many developments. For homeowners here, checking the specific subdivision's plat or CC&Rs before assuming a standard fence will meet requirements is a genuinely useful first step, since the requirement isn't uniform across every development in the area.

## Terrain considerations

Some Fortuna Foothills lots sit on more variable, foothill-adjacent terrain than the flatter ground closer to central Yuma. That can mean more grading or footing work for a perimeter wall, depending on the specific lot — another reason a proper quote should come from a contractor who's actually walked the property rather than priced it from a flat per-foot rate.
