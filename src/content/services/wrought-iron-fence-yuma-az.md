Ornamental metal fencing doesn't provide privacy, but it's the strongest curb-appeal option of the common materials, and its open-picket design doesn't catch wind the way a solid privacy panel does — a real advantage on an exposed Yuma lot during monsoon season.

## Where wrought iron and aluminum fit

Wrought iron is the heavier, more traditional choice, fabricated to almost any custom design and strong enough for higher-security applications. Aluminum delivers a very similar look for less money, with much better resistance to rust — worth weighing seriously given Yuma's monsoon-season humidity swings against otherwise hot, dry summers, which is exactly the kind of cycling that finds gaps in a painted finish over time.

## Iron or aluminum: what actually decides most jobs

| | Wrought iron | Ornamental aluminum |
|---|---|---|
| Rust | Will rust wherever the finish is breached | Does not rust |
| Upkeep | Inspect and touch up the finish; treat rust early | Essentially none beyond rinsing |
| Strength | Heavier; better for high-security applications | Adequate for residential use; dents rather than bends |
| Repair | Can be welded and re-worked | Usually a section replacement |
| Customization | Fabricated to nearly any design | Manufactured panel systems, fewer bespoke options |

For most residential fencing in Yuma, aluminum wins on cost and upkeep; iron wins where mass, strength, or a fully custom design is the point.

## Pool-barrier fit

Ornamental metal is a popular pool-enclosure choice precisely because it satisfies the required barrier without blocking sightlines to the water — but the panel has to actually be built to the spec. Arizona's pool-barrier law requires no opening wide enough to pass a 4-inch-diameter sphere; ask "is this panel rated for a pool barrier?" rather than assuming a decorative design automatically qualifies. ## Finish is most of the lifespan

With ornamental metal, what's on the metal matters as much as the metal itself:

- **Powder coat or wet paint?** Powder coating is baked on, thicker, and holds up far better under sustained UV than brushed or sprayed paint — standard on manufactured aluminum systems, and worth specifying on iron.
- **What happens at cut ends and welds?** On a fabricated iron fence, on-site cuts and welds break the factory finish. Ask how those are prepped and sealed — it's where corrosion typically starts.
- **What does the warranty actually cover?** Some finish warranties exclude fading or exclude corrosion starting at cut ends specifically — read that section before signing.

A once-a-year walk of the fence, checking the bottom few inches of pickets and posts on the side sprinklers hit, is most of the maintenance an ornamental fence actually needs.

## Height and setback rules

City of Yuma Municipal Code § 154-15.06 caps fencing at 3 feet in the front-yard setback citywide; taller ornamental fencing is generally allowed outside that setback. (YUM-005) Confirm the exact side/rear allowance and any canal-adjacent or subdivision-specific rule for your property with City of Yuma Building Safety.

## Gates carry more of the job than the panels

On an ornamental fence, gates take more cycles and more weight than anything else. A driveway gate is effectively its own project — wider leaves, heavier posts, deeper footings, and, if automated, power and safety sensors. See the [automatic & electric gate page](/services/automatic-gate-yuma-az/) if that's part of your project, and ask for gates to be priced as their own line item rather than folded into a per-foot rate.

## Vetting checklist

- Confirm the bid is under Arizona ROC **R-14 (Fencing)** — we work with providers who report being licensed and insured through the Arizona Registrar of Contractors, but verify any specific contractor's license directly at [roc.az.gov](https://roc.az.gov).
- Ask specifically whether the finish is powder-coated or painted, and how cut ends/welds are treated.
- If the fence needs to serve as a pool barrier, confirm picket spacing meets the 4-inch sphere rule in writing before work begins.
- Get gate posts and footings quoted separately from line posts — they carry more load and should be set differently.

## Red flags

- A quote that doesn't specify powder coat vs. paint on iron.
- A pool-adjacent panel sold as compliant without confirming picket spacing against the actual code.
- Gate pricing folded into the per-foot rate with no separate line item.
- Reluctance to provide an Arizona ROC license number, or one that comes back inactive at roc.az.gov.
