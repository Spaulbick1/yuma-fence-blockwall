Wood privacy fencing is the classic full-privacy option in Yuma — a lower upfront cost than vinyl or wrought iron, at the price of more regular upkeep against the desert sun.

## What's involved

A standard wood privacy fence installation includes setting posts in concrete (typically 24–30 inches deep, deeper at corners and gate posts), attaching rails, and hanging boards edge-to-edge or board-on-board for full privacy. Panel height commonly runs 4 feet for a front-yard/decorative fence up to 6 feet for backyard privacy or a pool enclosure.

Much of the Yuma area sits on caliche — a naturally cemented, calcium-carbonate hardpan common across the Sonoran Desert — which can slow post-hole digging considerably and require different equipment (an auger or jackhammer attachment rated for hardpan) than softer soil needs. Ask any contractor bidding the job whether they've priced for caliche after actually walking your yard, not from a phone estimate — hardpan is a common source of change-order surprises once digging starts.

## Cost factors

Beyond the base per-linear-foot price, several things move the total:

- **Style.** Standard privacy (boards butted edge to edge) is the cheapest. Board-on-board uses roughly a third more material and holds up better as wood shrinks in a dry climate. Shadowbox alternates boards on either side of the rails, finishing both faces and letting some wind through.
- **Height.** A 6-foot backyard privacy panel costs more per foot than a 4-foot front-yard picket-style panel.
- **Gate count and width.** Double-drive gates for RV or trailer access — common on Yuma's larger residential lots — add hardware and labor beyond the linear-footage rate.
- **Species.** Cedar and redwood cost more up front but hold a finish better and move less as they dry than pressure-treated pine, which matters more here than decay resistance does — the failure mode in a low-humidity climate is boards cupping and cracking, not rotting.
- **Removal of an existing fence.** Tear-out and haul-away of an old chain-link or wood fence is usually a separate line item.

## Desert-specific considerations

Yuma sees 4,000+ hours of sunshine a year and regularly sits above 100°F from June through September. Unsealed wood bleaches, dries, and can crack or cup noticeably faster here than in milder climates — plan on resealing or staining every couple of years as routine maintenance, not a one-time step after installation.

Arizona's monsoon season (roughly July–September) brings high, sudden wind gusts. A solid privacy fence presents a full face to that wind, so post depth, concrete cure time, and rail count matter more than the wood species itself for whether the fence stays standing through a bad monsoon storm. Ask a contractor what post depth and rail count they're quoting for an open, wind-exposed lot.

## Vetting checklist

- Confirm the bid is under Arizona ROC **R-14 (Fencing)** — we work with providers who report being licensed and insured through the Arizona Registrar of Contractors, but verify any specific contractor's license directly at [roc.az.gov](https://roc.az.gov) rather than taking a claim at face value.
- Get post depth, rail count, and concrete cure time in writing, especially on an open or wind-exposed lot.
- Confirm whether caliche/hardpan excavation is included in the quoted price or billed as an add-on if encountered.
- If the fence needs to enclose a pool, confirm the height, opening size, and gate hardware meet current Yuma pool-barrier requirements before work begins — see the [pool safety fencing page](/services/pool-fence-yuma-az/).
- Ask what species and fastener type (ring-shank or screw, not smooth nails) is being quoted, and what finish/sealing schedule the contractor recommends for this climate.

## Red flags

- A quote given without the contractor visiting the property, especially on a lot with unknown soil conditions.
- No mention of post depth, rail count, or concrete cure time.
- Reluctance to provide an Arizona ROC license number, or one that comes back inactive when checked at roc.az.gov.
- Pressure to sign same-day for a "discount."
