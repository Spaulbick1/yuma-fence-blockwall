A pool-barrier fence has one job an ordinary yard fence doesn't: satisfying a specific, code-defined safety standard, not just looking good or marking a property line. Arizona sets the statewide floor; the City of Yuma layers its own rule on top.

## Arizona's baseline requirement

State law ([A.R.S. § 36-1681](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/36/01681.htm)) applies to any outdoor pool or spa at least 18 inches deep and wider than 8 feet at any point. The core figures, direct from the statute:

- Barrier height **at least 5 feet**, measured on the exterior side.
- No opening large enough to pass a **4-inch-diameter sphere** — the test manufacturers and inspectors actually use.
- Barrier set back **at least 20 inches** from the water's edge.
- Gate opens **outward** from the pool, and is **self-closing and self-latching**, with the latch at least 54 inches above the ground (or, if the release sits lower, a padlock or keyed/combination lock).

The statute is a floor, not a ceiling — cities and counties can require more.

## City of Yuma's own rule — and where it's stricter

City of Yuma Municipal Code § 154-15.05 applies the same core barrier standard (5-foot fence/wall, no opening over 4 inches, self-closing/self-latching gates) to municipal, commercial, and private pools inside city limits, and adds:

- The pool must sit **at least 5 feet from side, rear, and street-side property lines**, respecting the underlying zoning district's front-yard setback.
- Pool equipment gets a **4-foot side setback** for fire access.
- **No exemption for all-adult households.** Arizona's state statute exempts a residence where every resident is at least six years old — City of Yuma's code does not carry that exemption forward. Inside city limits, don't assume a household's makeup removes the fencing requirement; confirm with City of Yuma Building Safety.

Worth flagging directly: a City of Yuma drowning-prevention guidance page repeats the national "48-inch" barrier figure alongside its own safety recommendations. That figure is *less strict* than Arizona's actual legal minimum of 5 feet (60 inches) — a barrier built to 48 inches does not meet the law that applies here.

Pools in unincorporated Yuma County areas (Fortuna Foothills, for example) fall under the state statute and Yuma County permitting instead of City of Yuma's municipal code — check with Yuma County Development Services for that jurisdiction's specific process.

## Material options for a pool barrier

Any of the materials on this site can be built to meet the barrier code, but each has a different tradeoff around the specific pool-safety spec:

- **Wood privacy.** Fully opaque and can hit the 5-foot minimum easily, but the gate's self-closing hardware needs periodic adjustment, and a solid panel needs no visible footholds on the pool side. See the [wood fence page](/services/wood-fence-yuma-az/).
- **Vinyl.** No footholds and a consistent factory height — a common, low-maintenance pool-barrier choice. See the [vinyl fence page](/services/vinyl-fence-yuma-az/).
- **Wrought iron or aluminum.** Preserves the view around the pool, but picket spacing has to actually satisfy the 4-inch sphere rule — a decorative panel chosen for looks alone isn't automatically pool-rated. See the [wrought iron & ornamental fence page](/services/wrought-iron-fence-yuma-az/).
- **Chain link.** The lowest-cost option, but standard chain-link mesh is climbable — ask specifically whether the mesh size and any added privacy slats still satisfy the 4-inch opening rule before assuming it qualifies. See the [chain-link fence page](/services/chain-link-fence-yuma-az/).

## The gate is where barriers actually fail

Panels rarely stop meeting code on their own. Gates do, because they carry moving hardware:

- **Self-closing hinges lose tension over time.** A gate that no longer swings shut on its own is no longer a barrier, even if the fence around it is perfect.
- **Latch height gets overlooked** on a retrofit gate added to an existing fence — confirm 54 inches (or the lower-latch alternative) after installation, not just on the spec sheet.
- **The gate must swing outward**, away from the pool — a detail sometimes missed when a gate is added to a fence built for a different purpose first.

## Vetting checklist

- Ask directly: "is this fence and gate rated to meet Arizona's pool-barrier code?" — not just "is it safe?"
- Confirm the bid is under Arizona ROC **R-14 (Fencing)** — we work with providers who report being licensed and insured through the Arizona Registrar of Contractors, but verify any specific contractor's license directly at [roc.az.gov](https://roc.az.gov).
- Get the barrier height, gate latch height, and setback from the water's edge confirmed in writing, specific to your property.
- If you're inside Yuma city limits, don't rely on the state's all-adult exemption — confirm requirements with City of Yuma Building Safety directly.

## Red flags

- A quote that doesn't mention gate hardware at all, or treats it as an afterthought.
- Anyone describing "48 inches" as a compliant height in Yuma.
- A contractor unwilling to confirm which code (state vs. City of Yuma) applies to your specific address.
- Pressure to reuse an existing yard fence as a pool barrier without checking whether it actually meets the sphere-opening and gate-latch requirements.
